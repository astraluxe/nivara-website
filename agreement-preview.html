Confidentiality (NDA)

Optional. Tick to see what each level covers. Ticking here only shows the clauses; nothing is saved or sent.

If documents are shared before the order, the same NDA is signed on its own first and carries into the agreement.

  1. What is confidential. Anything one side shares with the other for this project: documents, tender and supplier data, prices, workflows, source code, passwords and keys, whether marked confidential or clearly so.
  2. What is not. Information that is already public, was already known, is developed independently, is received lawfully from someone else, or must be disclosed by law or a court order (with notice to the other side where the law allows).
  3. Use. Only for this project, and only by the people who need it, who are bound by the same duty.
  4. Security. Each side protects the other's information with reasonable security practices (Information Technology Act, 2000, section 43A).
  5. Personal data. Personal data of the client's customers or staff is processed only on the client's instructions, as the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 requires.
  6. Return or deletion. On request, or when the work ends, copies are returned or deleted and this is confirmed in writing.
  7. How long. During the work and for 3 years after it ends; trade secrets and source code for as long as they stay confidential.
  8. No licence. Sharing information gives no ownership of it or right to use it for anything else.
  9. Remedies. A breach can be stopped by an injunction (Specific Relief Act, 1963) as well as claimed as damages.
  10. No non-compete. Neither side is stopped from doing its normal business; restraints of that kind are void in India (Indian Contract Act, 1872, section 27).

Under Indian law

  1. Governing law and courts. The agreement is governed by the laws of India. Disputes go first to arbitration in Bengaluru under the Arbitration and Conciliation Act, 1996, and otherwise to the courts in Bengaluru.
  2. Accepting online. An agreement accepted online is valid (Information Technology Act, 2000, section 10A). We also ask for a confirming email, so both sides have a clear record.
  3. Stamp duty. The signed agreement is stamped as the Karnataka Stamp Act, 1957 requires (by e-stamp); adris.tech arranges it.
  4. Authority. The person who accepts confirms they are authorised to sign for their company.
